हमारा कार्य · Four pillars

Programs that leave skills behind.

Everything we do follows one rule: when SSS leaves a village, the capacity stays. These are the four pillars of that promise.

Pillar 01 · Livelihoods

Skill Development & SHG Empowerment

SSS acts as a bridge to the future. We equip the youth and women of Bihar and Jharkhand with market-relevant trades — and take students where classrooms can't: onto real factory floors, from ITC Dairy in Munger to Shree Cement in Aurangabad.

  • Industrial exposure visits for students and teachers
  • Financial literacy and entrepreneurial leadership for SHG members
  • Certified training programs with CIPET and partner institutions
Pillar 02 · Governance

Panchayati Raj Orientation

Strengthening Panchayati Raj Institutions so local leaders become effective change-makers. Our multi-day residential trainings in district resource centres cover e-governance, new legal frameworks and citizen services — for Mukhiyas, Up-Mukhiyas, Nyaya Mitras, Kachahari Sachivs and more.

  • Two-day intensive programs with the Panchayati Raj Department
  • Coverage of e-Gram Kachahari and new criminal law provisions
  • Interactive Q&A that turns rules into working knowledge
Pillar 03 · Consumer rights

BIS Standards & Quality Awareness

In a unique initiative with the Bureau of Indian Standards, we teach citizens to demand quality: reading hallmarks on gold, spotting the ISI mark, and verifying products with the BIS CARE app. From school quiz competitions in Darbhanga to mass rallies of hundreds, awareness becomes protection.

  • Quiz competitions and standards clubs in schools
  • Hallmarking & HUID literacy sessions for consumers and jewellers
  • Manak Manthan industry workshops — from plastic pipes to waste management
Pillar 04 · WASH

Water, Sanitation & Hygiene

Aligned with the Swachh Bharat Mission, SSS has been instrumental in changing mindsets around sanitation. From constructing toilets to managing liquid and solid waste, we make sure swachhata becomes a way of life rather than a government mandate.

  • Village-level sanitation drives and toilet construction support
  • Liquid & solid waste management awareness
  • Water security education for every household
